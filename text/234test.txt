The Vision Behind CAFE 
The Capability Acceleration Framework for Enablement (CAFE) was created to unify Cisco’s partner 
enablement eorts across architectures, personas, and solutions. Like brewing the perfect espresso—
where the beans may vary but the process must be consistent. CAFE ensures a reliable, repeatable 
path to partner readiness. 
For internal Cisco enablement teams, CAFE enables alignment in the development of enablement 
assets, ensuring partner learning experiences are targeted, relevant, and consistent. 
 
 
Roles 
CAFE denes the following partner roles: 
• Business Sellers  
• Technical Sellers  
• Deployment Engineers 
By standardizing these personas and dening them based on action-oriented behaviors, CAFE 
ensures that Learning Journeys, Black Belt Learning Maps, and other enablement assets are grounded 
in real-world expectations and partner success. 
Business Sellers 
Business Sellers typically hold titles like Account Manager or Account Executive. They are focused on 
aligning Cisco’s solutions with customer business outcomes and driving strategic conversations.  The 
emphasis of this role, particularly at the higher stages, has a strong focus on consultative selling and 
alignment with business outcomes.  
Technical Sellers 
Technical Sellers are Pre-Sales Systems Engineers, Solutions Engineers, and Solutions Architects. 
They are responsible for the technical phase of the engagement, including solution design, validation, 
and PoC/PoV execution. As with Business Sellers, Technical Sellers are expected to develop more 
consultative and advisory capabilities, driving technology strategy and transformation with their 
customers and within their own organizations. 
CAFE is a role-based, architecture-agnostic skills framework organized around clearly 
articulated capabilities and metrics of success.  CAFE provides a consistent standard for 
learning outcomes that drive successful customer engagements across any Cisco solution.

Deployment Engineers 
Deployment Engineers are responsible for delivery of the solution, ensuring alignment between what 
was promised and what is delivered. Deployment Engineers have often been referred to as Field 
Engineers.  We are implementing more precise naming to dierentiate between engineering roles like 
Deployment Engineer and Operations Engineer. 
Maturity Stages 
The capabilities for each role are mapped to three progressive stages: 
• Stage 1: Foundational – “Engage” 
• Stage 2: Procient – “Propose & Deliver” 
• Stage 3: Focus & Depth – “Adopt & Expand” 
These stages build on familiar Cisco Partner Black Belt Academy foundations with increased precision 
and consistency.  Each stage is anchored on the success metrics that individuals are expected to 
demonstrate at each stage of their development. 
Stage 1: Foundational 
At Stage 1, the focus is on developing awareness and building condence. Individuals at this stage 
are expected to engage with customers, but are also expected to rely on the expertise of specialists 
or more experienced team members to some degree.  
The capabilities of this role are aligned with the requirements of a typical generalist.  Individuals at this 
level can navigate the breadth of Cisco’s core solutions and participate meaningfully in customer 
engagements, typically assisted by a specialist. 
Stage 2: Prociency 
At Stage 2, individuals take a more active role in building and validating solutions. Whether through 
developing business cases, designing and demonstrating technical architectures, or deploying 
production-grade solutions, these individuals are specialists within their domain.  They are prepared to 
dive deeper into specic technologies or verticals.  
Business and technical sellers are expected to take the lead role in proposing compelling solutions, 
developing and proving business and technical use cases.  Post-sales engineers are prepared to take 
the lead role in delivering those solutions into production. 
Stage 3: Focus & Depth 
At Stage 3, individuals develop specialized skills within their architectural domain and develop 
expertise in cross-architectural solutions.  They are also expected to shift into a leadership mindset, 
often leading and mentoring a team.

These individuals drive strategic outcomes by helping customers adopt Cisco solutions at scale and

How CAFE Powers Partner Enablement 
The core idea is simple: as Black Belt Learning Maps (BB LMs) and other training content are built for 
dierent Cisco solutions and architectures, the role requirements and metrics of success should 
remain consistent, regardless of persona or technology focus. 
CAFE provides a structure for delivering Partner Learning Journeys (PLJs). It guides partners through 
meaningful learning that aligns with real-world expectations and success outcomes. 
CAFE will serve as a standardized framework for Cisco content creation teams to ensure new learning 
assets are built with the partner experience in mind from the outset. 
Mapping CAFE Stages to Partner Role Denitions 
One of CAFE’s primary design objectives is to make it easier for partner individual contributors and 
leaders to identify the training that is best suited to their current and targeted level of expertise.  To 
that end, CAFE Stages 1 & 2 roles are aligned with typical “generalist” and “specialist” roles 
respectively. 
Of course, each partner or team within a partner organization may require dierent skill levels in 
dierent domains.  For example, Partner A might require a generalist or portfolio seller to demonstrate 
Prociency in core Networking, and Foundational in Security and Cloud+AI.  Partner B might require a 
generalist to demonstrate Prociency in Networking and Security, and Foundational in Collaboration, 
while Partner C’s generalists are Foundational in all architectures. 
Think of CAFE stages as bundles of skills that can be “assigned” to dierent roles within your 
organization.

The Deployment Engineer is the hands-on implementer who brings Cisco solutions to life in the 
customer environment. They are responsible for installing, conguring, and validating Cisco 
technologies across on-prem, cloud, or hybrid infrastructures. Their role is essential in bridging the 
gap between what was designed and what is delivered — ensuring technical accuracy, alignment to 
expectations, and real-world readiness.  
Deployment Engineers primarily operate in a post-sales capacity, but will often collaborate closely with 
Technical Sellers to turn proposed solutions into functional, tested implementations, often through 
controlled Proof of Value (PoV) engagements. Their success is measured not only by technical 
execution, but by their ability to translate design into impactful, value-driven outcomes that accelerate 
customer condence and adoption.
